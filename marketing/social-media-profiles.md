### r/relationships — Intro Post

**Title:** Anyone else spend more time deciding what to do than actually doing it? Here's what helped us

**Body:**
```
My partner and I realized we had a pattern: every Friday or Saturday, one of us would say "we should do something tonight" and then we'd spend an embarrassing amount of time in the "idk what do you want to do" spiral.

Then we'd get tired and just stay home. Again.

It wasn't a relationship problem — we both genuinely wanted to spend quality time together. It was a decision-making problem. Neither of us wanted to impose our idea on the other, so we'd both hedge, and nothing would get chosen.

A few things that actually helped us:

**1. The "two options" rule**
Instead of "what do you want to do?", one person offers exactly two options. The other picks one. Done. No blank-canvas paralysis.

**2. Themed nights**
We assigned rough themes to each week: one week is "try something new," next week is "cheap and cozy," the week after is "your choice, I'll plan everything." Reduces decision fatigue massively.

**3. The running list**
We keep a shared note of date ideas we want to try. When Saturday rolls around, we're not brainstorming from scratch — we're picking from a list we built together over time.

**4. The app approach**
We started using [DateNight](https://togetherplans.app) — it's free and basically gamifies the decision. You both propose ideas secretly, then vote, and the top choice wins. Feels weirdly fair and removes the "but I don't want to push my idea on you" anxiety.

Not every solution works for every couple. But if you're in the same loop we were, hopefully one of these helps.

What do you all do to make plans without it turning into a 45-minute negotiation? Genuinely curious what works for other people.
```
